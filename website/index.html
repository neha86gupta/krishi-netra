Project Vision & Purpose

Responsible AI for early crop leaf screening

Krishi Netra ("Vision for Agriculture") makes visual leaf disease screening understandable, bilingual, and transparent—while openly documenting where laboratory models succeed and where field conditions remain challenging.

The Agricultural Challenge

Why early recognition—and honest uncertainty—matter

Foliar diseases reduce crop yield and quality when left unnoticed until late stages. Smartphone-based leaf screening offers a fast way to spot visual symptoms early, yet many deep-learning demos present a single overconfident label with no explanation of how the model arrived at its answer.

In real agricultural settings, an uncalibrated, overconfident misdiagnosis can lead to wasted inputs or incorrect crop care. Krishi Netra addresses this directly by pairing a lightweight MobileNetV3 classifier with confidence calibration, Grad-CAM visual heatmaps, and static source-linked care notes.

Rather than replacing local agronomists, Krishi Netra acts as an initial screening and educational companion that directs growers to their nearest Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK) or agriculture officer before taking treatment action.

Core Principles

What makes Krishi Netra distinct

  • Single-Leaf Clarity: Built around photographing one leaf clearly in daylight rather than guessing on wide, ambiguous field scenes.
  • Right to Abstain ("Not Sure"): Calibrated confidence thresholds prevent forced guesses when lighting, angle, or disease symptoms are unclear.
  • Separation of ML and Advice: Visual pattern classification is powered by MobileNetV3, whereas care notes come strictly from a static, source-linked reference database—clearly labelled as not machine learning.
  • Bilingual Accessibility: Full workflow support in both English and Hindi.
  • Field-Proven Benchmarks: Reports verified 95.8% lab precision and 90.3% outdoor field F1 score side by side.

Built to serve real Indian farmers with practical, trustworthy, and explainable AI — not just theoretical benchmarks.

Zero Cloud — Fully Offline Capable

Practical Field Guide for Farmers

How to Photograph a Leaf for Best Results

Four simple guidelines that maximize diagnostic accuracy from the neural network model.

✅ Do's (Recommended Practice)

  • Diffuse Daylight: Early morning or late afternoon light prevents harsh glare on leaves.
  • Single Leaf Focus: Fill 70% of the frame with one leaf blade showing visible disease spots.
  • Steady Close-Up: Hold phone 15–20 cm away and tap screen to lock focus on the lesion area.
  • Flat Angle: Keep camera parallel to the leaf surface to reveal concentric fungal rings clearly.

❌ Don'ts (Avoid These)

  • Flash Photography: LED flash at night causes specular reflections that mask disease spots.
  • Cluttered Clusters: Avoid photographing tangled multi-leaf bushes or entire plant canopies.
  • Motion Blur: Do not capture leaves swaying in strong wind; wait for still conditions.
  • Soil/Hand Obstruction: Avoid covering the diseased leaf area with fingers or mud.

Neural Pipeline Breakdown

The 6-Stage Inference Pipeline

How one mobile photograph travels from pixel normalization through MobileNetV3 feature extraction, Grad-CAM backpropagation, and calibrated uncertainty gating to reach a diagnosis.

Stage 1

Bilinear Normalization

Resizes to 224x224 and normalizes RGB using ImageNet mean [0.485, 0.456, 0.406] and std [0.229, 0.224, 0.225].

Stage 2

Depthwise Convolutions

MobileNetV3-Large inverted bottlenecks with Squeeze-and-Excitation attention extract fine leaf lesion textures efficiently.

Stage 3

38-Class Logit Scoring

Linear classifier maps latent feature vectors into 38 plant-disease class energy scores.

Stage 4

Temperature Calibration

Applies temperature scaling (T=1.0) and entropy thresholding to align softmax probabilities with true empirical accuracy.

Stage 5

Grad-CAM Attribution

Backpropagates gradients through the final convolutional layer to generate 2D spatial heatmap coordinates showing what the model "sees".

Stage 6

KVK Knowledge Lookup

Queries bilingual agronomic knowledge base for organic biopesticides, chemical controls, and nearest KVK referral number.

System Architecture & Stack

Lightweight vision architecture with built-in explainability

Krishi Netra pairs a compact MobileNetV3 neural backbone with post-hoc confidence calibration, Grad-CAM saliency attribution, and a decoupled static knowledge base.

Interactive Pipeline Inspector

End-to-end inference & attribution flow

Select any architectural stage below to inspect its role and design safeguards.

Stage 01 · Input & Preprocessing

Single-Leaf Image Capture & Normalization

Accepts user-uploaded JPG/PNG images or live device camera captures. Images are resized and normalized for MobileNetV3 inference while guiding users to frame one focused leaf in good daylight.

Formats: JPG / PNG · Guidance: Single leaf, daylight illumination, sharp focus

Stage 02 · Neural Backbone

MobileNetV3 Multi-Class Classifier (38 Classes)

MobileNetV3 uses depthwise separable convolutions, inverted residuals, and squeeze-and-excitation blocks—delivering strong visual feature extraction with low computational overhead.

Training Corpus (V1): PlantVillage · Output Head: 38 crop & disease classes

Stage 03 · Reliability Layer

Probability Calibration & "Not Sure" Abstention

Calibrates predicted class probabilities so scores reflect true empirical likelihood and applies an uncertainty threshold so low-confidence inputs return an explicit "not sure" response.

Output: Ranked Top-3 classes + Calibrated Confidence % or "Not Sure" abstention

Stage 04 · Visual Explainability

Grad-CAM Spatial Attribution Overlay

Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM) projects target-class gradients onto the final convolutional feature maps to highlight which image regions drove the prediction—helping users detect background distraction.

Purpose: Audit background bias & build human interpretability

Stage 05 · Deterministic Reference Layer

Decoupled Static Care Knowledge Base & KVK Referral

Care notes are retrieved from a static, source-linked knowledge base rather than generated by machine learning, and are clearly labelled as not machine learning alongside Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK) guidance.

Localization: English & Hindi UI + Diagnosis History & Unseen Demo Mode

Complete Disease Reference Library

38-Class Disease Encyclopedia

Click any disease to expand detailed symptoms, pathogen information, and recommended care. Krishi Netra detects all 38 classes listed below.

🍅 Tomato — Early Blight (Alternaria solani)

▼

Symptoms: Dark brown concentric rings ("target spots") on older lower leaves, spreading upward. Leaves yellow and drop prematurely.

Pathogen: Fungal — Alternaria solani. Thrives in warm, humid conditions (24–29°C).

Care: Remove infected leaves immediately. Apply Mancozeb 75% WP (2.5 g/L) or neem oil (5 ml/L) every 7–10 days. Rotate crops annually.

🍅 Tomato — Late Blight (Phytophthora infestans)

▼

Symptoms: Water-soaked grey-green lesions on leaves and stems. White fuzzy growth on underside in humid conditions. Rapid wilting.

Pathogen: Oomycete — Phytophthora infestans. Spreads explosively in cool wet weather (15–22°C).

Care: Destroy infected plants immediately. Apply Metalaxyl + Mancozeb (Ridomil Gold) at first sign. Avoid overhead irrigation.

🍅 Tomato — Septoria Leaf Spot (Septoria lycopersici)

▼

Symptoms: Many small circular spots (1–3 mm) with dark borders and grey centers on lower leaves. Tiny black dots (pycnidia) visible in center.

Pathogen: Fungal — Septoria lycopersici. Favours warm, rainy weather.

Care: Remove lower infected leaves. Apply Chlorothalonil (2 g/L) or copper-based fungicide. Mulch around base to prevent splash.

🍅 Tomato — Bacterial Spot (Xanthomonas vesicatoria)

▼

Symptoms: Small, dark, water-soaked spots on leaves and fruit. Spots may have yellow halos. Leaves become ragged.

Pathogen: Bacterial — Xanthomonas vesicatoria. Spread by rain splash and contaminated seed.

Care: Use disease-free certified seeds. Apply copper hydroxide spray. Avoid working in wet fields. Remove infected debris.

🍅 Tomato — Target Spot (Corynespora cassiicola)

▼

Symptoms: Tan-brown lesions with concentric zonation on leaves. Distinct from early blight by lighter color and irregular shape.

Pathogen: Fungal — Corynespora cassiicola. Favours warm humid tropical climates.

Care: Improve air circulation with proper spacing. Apply Azoxystrobin or Difenoconazole at recommended doses.

🍅 Tomato — Yellow Leaf Curl Virus (TYLCV)

▼

Symptoms: Upward curling and yellowing of leaf margins. Plants stunted with small, deformed fruit. Severe yield loss.

Pathogen: Viral — Tomato Yellow Leaf Curl Virus, transmitted by whiteflies (Bemisia tabaci).

Care: Use TYLCV-resistant varieties. Control whiteflies with yellow sticky traps and neem oil. Remove infected plants early.

🍅 Tomato — Mosaic Virus (ToMV)

▼

Symptoms: Mottled light and dark green patches on leaves. Leaves may be distorted, curled, or fernlike. Reduced fruit set.

Pathogen: Viral — Tomato Mosaic Virus. Transmitted mechanically via hands, tools, and infected seed.

Care: No chemical cure. Use resistant varieties. Sterilize tools with 10% bleach. Wash hands before handling plants.

🍅 Tomato — Spider Mite Damage (Tetranychus urticae)

▼

Symptoms: Fine yellow stippling on upper leaf surfaces. Tiny webbing visible on undersides. Leaves become bronzed and dry.

Pathogen: Arachnid pest — Two-spotted spider mite. Thrives in hot, dry conditions.

Care: Spray strong water jets to dislodge mites. Apply neem oil or insecticidal soap. Introduce predatory mites (Phytoseiulus persimilis).

🍅 Tomato — Leaf Mold (Passalora fulva)

▼

Symptoms: Pale green-yellow spots on upper leaf surface with olive-green velvety mold on the underside. Primarily affects greenhouse tomatoes.

Pathogen: Fungal — Passalora fulva (formerly Cladosporium fulvum). High humidity >85% required.

Care: Improve ventilation, reduce humidity. Use resistant cultivars. Apply Chlorothalonil preventively.

🍅 Tomato — Healthy ✅

▼

Indicators: Uniform deep green color. No spots, lesions, yellowing, or curling. Strong stem attachment. Active new leaf growth.

Care: Continue balanced NPK fertilization, adequate irrigation, and pest monitoring for sustained health.

🥔 Potato — Early Blight (Alternaria solani)

▼

Symptoms: Dark brown target-like spots with concentric rings on older leaves. Progresses upward causing defoliation.

Pathogen: Fungal — Alternaria solani. Same pathogen as tomato early blight.

Care: Practice 2–3 year crop rotation. Apply Mancozeb or Chlorothalonil. Hill soil around plants to protect tubers.

🥔 Potato — Late Blight (Phytophthora infestans)

▼

Symptoms: Water-soaked dark green-black lesions spreading rapidly. White sporulation on leaf undersides. Tubers show reddish-brown rot.

Pathogen: Oomycete — Phytophthora infestans. The pathogen that caused the Irish Potato Famine.

Care: Use certified disease-free seed potatoes. Apply Metalaxyl+Mancozeb immediately at first sign. Destroy all infected material.

🥔 Potato — Healthy ✅

▼

Indicators: Rich green foliage with no spots or wilting. Firm tuber skin with no lesions or soft rot.

Care: Maintain proper soil moisture and hilling. Monitor for pests weekly during growing season.

🍎 Apple — Apple Scab (Venturia inaequalis)

▼

Symptoms: Olive-green to brown velvety spots on leaves and fruit. Severe cases cause leaf curling and premature drop. Fruit develops rough, corky lesions.

Pathogen: Fungal — Venturia inaequalis. Overwinters in fallen leaves.

Care: Rake and destroy fallen leaves in autumn. Apply fungicide (Captan or Myclobutanil) from green tip through petal fall.

🍎 Apple — Black Rot (Botryosphaeria obtusa)

▼

Symptoms: "Frog-eye" leaf spots — tan center with dark brown borders. Fruit develops large brown rotting area with concentric rings. Cankers on branches.

Pathogen: Fungal — Botryosphaeria obtusa.

Care: Prune out cankers and dead wood. Remove mummified fruit. Apply Captan during growing season.

🍎 Apple — Cedar Apple Rust (Gymnosporangium juniperi-virginianae)

▼

Symptoms: Bright orange-yellow spots on upper leaf surface. Tubular structures (aecia) form on underside. Requires alternate juniper/cedar host.

Pathogen: Fungal — Gymnosporangium juniperi-virginianae.

Care: Remove nearby cedar/juniper trees if possible. Apply Myclobutanil at pink bud stage. Use resistant varieties.

🍎 Apple — Healthy ✅

▼

Indicators: Glossy green leaves with smooth surface. No spots, discoloration, or curling. Clean fruit development.

Care: Continue regular pruning, balanced fertilization, and dormant season sprays for prevention.

🌽 Corn — Common Rust (Puccinia sorghi)

▼

Symptoms: Small, round to elongated cinnamon-brown pustules on both leaf surfaces. Pustules break open releasing powdery rust-colored spores.

Pathogen: Fungal — Puccinia sorghi. Favours moderate temperatures (16–25°C) with heavy dew.

Care: Plant resistant hybrids. Fungicide (Propiconazole) application warranted only for severe early-season infections on sweet corn.

🌽 Corn — Northern Leaf Blight (Exserohilum turcicum)

▼

Symptoms: Long (3–15 cm), elliptical grey-green lesions on leaves, typically starting from lower canopy. Can significantly reduce yield if infection occurs before tasseling.

Pathogen: Fungal — Exserohilum turcicum.

Care: Use Ht-resistant varieties. Apply Azoxystrobin at VT/R1 stage if conditions favor disease spread. Rotate with non-host crops.

🌽 Corn — Grey Leaf Spot (Cercospora zeae-maydis)

▼

Symptoms: Rectangular grey lesions running parallel to leaf veins. Lesions are distinctly angular, unlike the elliptical shape of NLB.

Pathogen: Fungal — Cercospora zeae-maydis. Favours prolonged high humidity and warm nights.

Care: Rotate crops and till residue. Plant tolerant hybrids. Foliar fungicide (Trifloxystrobin) at VT if warranted.

🌽 Corn — Healthy ✅

▼

Indicators: Vibrant green leaves with no lesions or discoloration. Strong stalk and proper ear development.

Care: Adequate nitrogen fertilization, proper plant spacing, and timely irrigation ensure maximum yield.

🍇 Grape — Black Rot (Guignardia bidwellii)

▼

Symptoms: Tan circular leaf spots with dark margins. Fruit shrivels into hard, black mummified berries. Can destroy entire crop.

Pathogen: Fungal — Guignardia bidwellii.

Care: Remove all mummified fruit and infected canes during dormant pruning. Apply Mancozeb or Myclobutanil from bud break to veraison.

🍇 Grape — Esca (Black Measles) (Phaeomoniella + Phaeoacremonium)

▼

Symptoms: Interveinal "tiger stripe" pattern — dark spots between leaf veins. Berries develop dark spots. Chronic decline over years.

Pathogen: Fungal complex — multiple wood-colonizing fungi infecting through pruning wounds.

Care: Protect pruning wounds with fungicidal paste. Delay pruning until late in dormancy. Remove severely affected vines.

🍇 Grape — Isariopsis Leaf Spot (Pseudocercospora vitis)

▼

Symptoms: Angular dark brown spots on leaves, often with yellow halos. Dark sporulation visible on underside.

Pathogen: Fungal — Pseudocercospora vitis (syn. Isariopsis clavispora).

Care: Apply copper-based fungicide. Improve canopy air circulation through pruning. Remove fallen leaf debris.

🍇 Grape — Healthy ✅

▼

Indicators: Bright green leaves with no spots or tiger-stripe patterns. Good fruit cluster development.

Care: Maintain balanced canopy management, regular scouting, and timely harvest.

🌶️ Pepper — Bacterial Spot (Xanthomonas campestris)

▼

Symptoms: Small, raised, water-soaked spots on leaves and fruit. Spots turn brown and scabby. Severe defoliation in wet weather.

Pathogen: Bacterial — Xanthomonas campestris pv. vesicatoria.

Care: Use certified disease-free transplants. Apply copper hydroxide + Mancozeb. Avoid overhead watering. 2-year crop rotation.

🌶️ Pepper — Healthy ✅

▼

Indicators: Uniform dark green leaves with no water-soaked spots. Vigorous fruit setting and canopy growth.

Care: Balanced fertilization, drip irrigation, and regular pest scouting maintain plant vigor.

🍒 Cherry — Powdery Mildew (Podosphaera clandestina)

▼

Symptoms: White powdery coating on leaves, especially young growth. Leaves curl and distort. Fruit may develop russeted patches.

Pathogen: Fungal — Podosphaera clandestina. Dry, warm days with cool nights favor it.

Care: Improve air circulation with proper pruning. Apply sulfur-based fungicide or Myclobutanil at first sign.

🍒 Cherry — Healthy (incl. Sour) ✅

▼

Indicators: Clean glossy leaves with no powder or spots. Normal fruit coloring and ripening progression.

Care: Dormant-season pruning and copper spray prevent common diseases for the following season.

🍑 Peach — Bacterial Spot (Xanthomonas arboricola)

▼

Symptoms: Angular dark spots on leaves, often causing "shot-hole" appearance. Fruit develops shallow, dark, sunken lesions.

Pathogen: Bacterial — Xanthomonas arboricola pv. pruni.

Care: Plant resistant cultivars. Apply Oxytetracycline during bloom. Copper sprays at leaf fall help reduce inoculum.

🍑 Peach — Healthy ✅

▼

Indicators: Smooth, elongated leaves with uniform green color. No angular spots or shot-hole patterns.

Care: Annual dormant pruning and balanced nutrition. Monitor for oriental fruit moth and peach leaf curl.

🍓 Strawberry — Leaf Scorch (Diplocarpon earlianum)

▼

Symptoms: Irregular dark purple blotches on upper leaf surface. Spots merge, causing leaves to appear "scorched" and brown. Distinct from leaf spot by lack of defined center.

Pathogen: Fungal — Diplocarpon earlianum.

Care: Use resistant varieties. Remove infected leaves after harvest. Apply Captan or Thiram as preventive foliar spray.

🍓 Strawberry — Healthy ✅

▼

Indicators: Trifoliate leaves with bright green, firm leaflets. No purple spots or marginal browning. Active runner production.

Care: Adequate mulching, drip irrigation, and renovation pruning maintain long-term bed health.

🎃 Squash — Powdery Mildew (Podosphaera xanthii)

▼

Symptoms: White powdery patches on upper leaf surfaces that expand rapidly. Severely affected leaves turn brown and die. Common in late season.

Pathogen: Fungal — Podosphaera xanthii.

Care: Plant PM-resistant varieties. Apply potassium bicarbonate or sulfur at first sign. Avoid excess nitrogen fertilization.

🍊 Orange — Huanglongbing (Citrus Greening) (Candidatus Liberibacter)

▼

Symptoms: Asymmetric yellow mottling on leaves ("blotchy mottle"). Lopsided, bitter, green-at-stem-end fruit. Tree decline over 3–5 years.

Pathogen: Bacterial — Candidatus Liberibacter asiaticus. Vectored by Asian citrus psyllid.

Care: No cure exists. Control psyllid vector aggressively with systemic insecticide. Remove symptomatic trees to slow spread. Use certified disease-free nursery stock.

🫘 Soybean — Healthy ✅

▼

Indicators: Trifoliate leaves uniformly green with no lesions, rust pustules, or mosaic patterns. Vigorous nodulation.

Care: Inoculate seeds with Bradyrhizobium. Monitor for soybean rust and sudden death syndrome in humid regions.

Common Inquiries

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about offline use, accuracy, and educational deployment.

🌾 Does Krishi Netra work without internet in remote farms?

Yes — the full 17.2 MB MobileNetV3 model, Grad-CAM engine, and knowledge base run entirely on local CPU. Zero active network connection is needed during leaf inference.

🛡️ What happens when the AI is not confident about a photo?

Unlike cloud APIs that make forced wrong guesses, Krishi Netra applies a temperature-calibrated confidence gate. If confidence falls below 70%, it outputs a "Not Sure" abstention and advises consulting the nearest KVK.

🔬 How is the infection severity percentage calculated?

Computer vision contour analysis measures the total necrotic surface area of the infected leaf tissue relative to the total healthy green leaf area and outputs a percentage estimate.

🏫 Can schools, colleges, and cooperatives use Krishi Netra?

Absolutely. Built on open Python/PyTorch/Streamlit standards and CC-licensed training data, it is ideal for agriculture science fairs, university research demonstrations, and rural cooperative field scouting.
